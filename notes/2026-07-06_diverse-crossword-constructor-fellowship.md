> Re: #1: For crossword construction, I currently work off "secondary resources", e.g. Patrick Berry's guide and the Crosswords Discord community. I've been able to learn quite well from those, having my first crossword accepted several months after starting the hobby. However, I feel like there's a limit to far I can reach with these types of resources alone. Even when I get feedback from editors, it's on the order of a week at minimum and months normally. The rate of feedback makes it difficult for me to be confident in my experiences. I'm applying because I want to get firsthand experience working with the NYT crossword editing team. Furthermore, by working with the NYT crossword team, I'm hoping I'll be able to gain new perspective on how to think about novel crossword themes, an area I find myself lacking.
> 
> Re. #2: I like to bring fresh takes on themes/cluing angles to my crosswords, and for this purpose, I'll mine Wikipedia/Wiktionary/TV Tropes. I'm a bit of a nerd in a variety of spaces (media analysis, digital art, Pokemon, cats, etc.), so knowledge from domains like these come through in my cluing.
> 
> Disclaimer: While I have not had a submission _published_ by the NYT, I've had a submission _accepted_. I thought I should be upfront about this—I'm still interested in this fellowship as a way to learn!
