> With regards to this puzzle: it's a cute idea, but despite this we're going to turn it down. This was a pretty straightforward no for us, cute as the premise is, for a couple tightly connected reasons:
> 
> - The most popular crossword solving applet, Across Lite, straight-up doesn't allow the solver to enter underscores into the white squares, so this puzzle is technically unsolvable as is. This wouldn't usually be a problem -- just write BLANK in those entries in lieu of the five underscores -- but that results in a different issue...
> - There's some precedent for grids like these, and in those grids, the key to the puzzles is that the entries make sense with or without the blanked out words. In your puzzle, when you *do* write BLANK in at 3-Down, the entries crossing it are ABBS, LELTS, ETAA, CANRDIO, and KTRAP, all of which are nonsense; for us to take a puzzle like this we'd need the crossing entries to be things like (e.g.) LOBO clued as LOO, POLE clued as POE, etc.
> - And finally, Ben notes that there's quite a few other puzzles with a similar execution to this one (as in, either titled "Mind the Gap" or with MIND THE GAP in the grid), and as such, we'd want a puzzle using this mechanism to have a rationale for the removed letters beyond just that they're literal blanks. A nice example of that would be, to use a recent indie puzzle I really liked, this Will Nediger grid called "Losing My Religion" (https://blog.bewilderinglypuzzles.com/2026/03/puzzle-260-losing-my-religion.html) where the absent letters are all religions (so the crossing entries ignore the SHINTO part of CRASH INTO).
> 
> So, alas, this will be a no. I did really like the grid shape here, which is very aesthetically pleasing, and there's some neat clues that avoid all the novice pitfalls. The ZAPPA/OCEAN clue repetition is very cool, and there's also a bit of tricksiness here -- I'm 50/50 on whether this was intentional or a fun coincidence -- at 60-Across, where [___ Tzu] is the clue for what should be a four-letter entry (and thus SHIH), but what is actually a three-letter entry (SUN).
